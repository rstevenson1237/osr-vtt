WI-26 — Presence & Seat Lifecycle

Mockup gate for WI-26 (spec R26, Master Plan Part III). Three UI-affecting surfaces: the Players panel, map token dimming, and the Session → Maintenance prune block.

The governing idea is R26.2's table: disconnected is a display state and carries no data consequence whatsoever. Nothing on these boards deletes anything, moves anything, or changes a seat. A player who shuts their laptop for a week comes back to their seat, their character and their tokens exactly as they left them. Only Board 3 touches data, and only when the referee presses the button.

Board 1 Players panel — present vs. disconnected

One dot per row, left of the name. Filled green = presence node exists and its ts is within 2× the 45 s heartbeat. Hollow grey = absent or stale; the whole row drops to 55% opacity and gains a relative "last seen". Every existing control (Rename, role, Make referee, Remove) stays exactly where it is and stays enabled — a disconnected player is still a player, and the referee may still administer their seat.

Board 2 Map — token dimming for a disconnected player

A token whose owning seat has no live presence renders at ~42% opacity with reduced saturation, plus a small hollow badge on the token's lower-right. The status ring (R21) keeps its colour and full weight, so "whose token is this" and "is that player here" stay independent readings rather than competing for the same channel.

Deliberately not proposed: hiding a disconnected player's token, moving it, or greying its ring. The token stays exactly where it is and stays draggable by anyone who could already move it — a groupmate covering for someone who dropped is the normal case at a table, not an edge case to design against.

Board 3 Session → Maintenance — prune inactive seats

A new block in the existing danger zone, beside prune-entries and delete-room. It lists only seats whose lastPresentAt is older than 30 days, each with a checkbox, and reuses the seat-removal path the Players panel already uses — including its "also delete character sheet" option, which stays off by default: a player gone a month may still have a character the referee wants to keep.

Open question for approval

Board 1's inactive pill is the one element not called for by R26 — the spec puts abandoned seats in Maintenance only. It is included because the referee reading the Players list is the person who would act on it, and omitting it means the only way to discover a dead seat is to open the danger zone. Say the word and it comes out; nothing else on these boards depends on it.
