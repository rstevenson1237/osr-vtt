## 1. The table — ten tools × three snap modes

"Anchor" is the function that decides *where* the gesture's points land. "Shape class"
changes when the committed geometry is a different **kind** of shape under one mode than
another (round brush vs cell union, round cap vs flat cap) — as opposed to the same shape
merely quantized differently.

| Tool         | Cell (`full`)                                                              | Half (`half`)                        | Free (`free`)                                                         | Shape class changes with mode? |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **Room**     | `snapCell` on both raw corners, whole-cell inclusive rect (`cellRectPoly`) | same, ½ step                         | raw corners, `rectPoly` corner-to-corner                              | **No** — axis-aligned rect in every mode |
| **Corridor** | `snapCellCenter` per end (`bandLo`/`bandSpan`), + `cornerBlock`            | same, ½ step                         | **raw** ends; `bandLo` = `centre − w/2`, `bandSpan` = raw `[a,b]`     | **No** — flat-capped axis-aligned rectangles in every mode |
| **Path**     | `snapCellCenter` per click → Corridor legs + `cornerBlock`                 | same, ½ step                         | `bufferPolyline` — round caps, round joins                            | **Yes** — cited, SPEC-028 §7 |
| **Polygon**  | `snapPoint` (nearest lattice **vertex**)                                   | same, ½ step                         | raw + **vertex attraction** (§12)                                     | **No** |
| **N-gon**    | centre `snapCellCenter`; span `snapSpan` (≥ 1 cell); angle `snapAngle` (4 pts) | same (≥ ½ cell; 8 pts)            | raw centre, **raw span (no floor)**, raw angle                        | **No** — regular polygon / circle in every mode |
| **Carve**    | raw samples → `snapCellCenter`, union of whole cells whose centre is within `max(w/2, step/2)` | same, ½ cells       | `bufferPolyline` — round ribbon of exactly `width`                    | **Yes** — cited, DEC-032 |
| **Wall**     | `snapPoint` (vertex)                                                       | same, ½ step                         | raw + **vertex attraction**                                           | **No** |
| **Door**     | `snapPoint` (vertex)                                                       | same, ½ step                         | raw + **vertex attraction**                                           | **No** |
| **Symbol**   | `anchorCellFor` → `snapCell` (floor, not round)                            | same, ½ step                         | raw, **no attraction**                                                | **No** |
| **Label**    | `snapCell` (floor, not round)                                              | same, ½ step                         | raw, **no attraction**                                                | **No** |

### The structural answer the table gives

**There are three anchor families, not two.** The spec's §2 framing is "cell-anchored vs
vertex-snapped", but the code has a third:

| Anchor          | Function          | Tools                              | Free's vertex attraction? |
| --------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Lattice vertex  | `snapPoint`       | Wall, Door, Polygon                | **Yes** (§12)              |
| Cell **centre** | `snapCellCenter`  | Corridor, Path, N-gon, Carve       | No                         |
| Cell **corner** | `snapCell` (floor)| Room, Symbol, Label                | No                         |

`CELL_ANCHORED_TOOLS` (`vector-tools.ts:290`) merges the last two, because from
`buildFloorStroke`'s point of view they share one property — points arrive raw. That is
correct for the plumbing and incomplete as a description of behaviour: Room floors to a
corner, its four list-mates centre on a cell, and Symbol and Label floor to a corner
without being on the list at all (they are not `FloorPrimitiveTool`s, so they take their
own path through `placeSymbolAt` / `placeLabelAt`).

**Only two tools change shape class with the snap mode**, and both are cited. Every other
per-mode difference is quantization — which is the reassuring half of IN-085's question.

### Cited, and confirmed against the code

| C2 | **Path**: flat squared caps under Cell/Half, round caps under Free | SPEC-028 §7 "Terminations" | Holds **for Path only** — see F2 |
| C3 | **Indicators**: Room's targeted-cell highlight absent under Free; Corridor/Path's band indicator becomes a circle under Free | SPEC-028 §6 (WI-052 amendment) | Holds as described — but the circle is wrong for the Corridor, see F1 |

### Not cited — these are the deliverable

Each becomes its own intake item (IN-095 – IN-103), logged in `INTAKE.md` §1.1 as **Open**
with a proposed classification, awaiting the user's approval of the classification.

**F1 — The Corridor's Free indicator advertises a round cap the Corridor never draws.**
`targetedBandFor` (`vector-tools.ts:370`) returns `{ kind: 'circle' }` under Free for
**corridor *and* path**, justified in its own doc comment and in SPEC-028 §6 as "matching
the round cap a free-snap Path produces". But `corridorPoly` (`primitives.ts:372`) has no
Free branch at all: every mode goes through `bandRect`, and `bandSpan`'s Free branch
(`primitives.ts:278`) returns the raw `[a, b]` — flat caps, square joints, always. So of
the two tools that indicator serves, one rounds and one does not, and under Free the
Corridor's indicator is a circle in front of a rectangle. Cosmetic, but it is the tool
telling the referee the wrong thing about the shape they are about to commit.

**F2 — §7's "flat vs round cap" outlier is Path's alone.** SPEC-028 §7's Terminations
paragraph, and IN-085's own finding (2), both attribute the cap change to "Corridor and
Path". The Corridor never rounds in any mode. Its real Free difference is narrower and
duller: unquantized endpoints and an unquantized band centre. Same root cause as F1, but a
separate doc edit (§7 and §6 both need the correction, as does IN-085's rationale text —
which is preserved as written per `INTAKE.md`'s reading note, so the correction belongs in
the spec).

**F3 — The snapped Carve dab is a Euclidean disc of cells, not a block.** `buildBrushStroke`
(`vector-tools.ts:483`) paints every cell whose *centre* is within `radius` of the anchored
path, `radius = max(width / 2, step / 2)`. Under Cell snap a single dab therefore gives:
width ≤ 1 → 1 cell; width 2 (radius 1) → the anchor **plus its four cardinal neighbours, a
plus, not a 2×2 or 3×3 block**; width 3 (radius 1.5) → the full 3×3, because the diagonals
sit at √2 ≈ 1.414. The footprint alternates plus-shaped and square-ish as the width climbs.
SPEC-028 §2's WI-042 note calls it "a block", which is what a referee would expect and not
what the code does.

**F4 — Carve widths 0.5 and 1.0 are indistinguishable under Cell snap.** The `step / 2`
radius floor in the same line. `MapToolbar`'s Width control is `min="0.5" step="0.5"`
(`MapToolbar.svelte:359-366`), so the control's first two stops are the same stroke under
`full`. Under `half` the floor is 0.25 and the minimum width is 0.5, so nothing collapses
there. The floor is deliberate (IN-012: a sub-cell brush that committed nothing); the
resulting dead stop on the control is not documented anywhere.

**F5 — Symbol and Label show the Snap selector and give no snap feedback whatsoever.**
`MapToolbar`'s `SNAP_TOOLS` includes `label` and `symbol` (`MapToolbar.svelte:250`, from
IN-057/WI-075), but `VectorMapView`'s `SNAP_CURSOR_TOOLS` (`VectorMapView.svelte:338`)
excludes both, and `targetedCellFor` is Room-only by construction
(`vector-tools.ts:332-345`). So the two tools whose placement *is* "which cell did you
click" are the only snap-mode tools with neither a snap dot nor a cell highlight. The
`SNAP_CURSOR_TOOLS` comment explains the exclusion as "`symbol` places by cell-floor, not
vertex-snap" — true, and an argument for giving it Room's *cell* indicator rather than for
giving it nothing.

**F6 — Under Free, Symbol and Label store an unquantized `cell` anchor.**
`snapCell(p, 'free')` returns the raw point (`snap.ts:91`), so `MapSymbol.cell` and
`MapRoom.labelAnchor` become arbitrary lattice floats. RULE-006-legal — lattice units are
floats and nothing is stored in pixels — and almost certainly intended, but the field is
named `cell` and `anchorCellFor`'s doc calls it "the cell … containing the raw pointer
position", which reads as a cell address. A doc note, not a change.

**F7 — SPEC-028 §6's dot rule contradicts itself, and the code follows the second half.**
The WI-048 amendment says the dot "is drawn _in addition to_ the cell highlight … so Room
under Cell or Half snap shows a dot in the middle of the tile it already highlights", then
two sentences later says "Where a tile or shape indicator supersedes the point, the point
is no longer drawn." `vector-engine.ts:1895` implements the second:
`if (input.cursorSnap && !input.cursorCell && !input.cursorBand)`. Room shows **no** dot;
N-gon, Carve, Wall, Door and Polygon keep theirs. The first sentence was superseded by
WI-052 and never struck.

**F8 — The one-cell minimum is a snapped-mode-only guarantee, and Free's five answers to
"a click with no drag" disagree with each other.** Under Cell/Half every floor tool commits
exactly one cell for a zero-length gesture (`snapSpan`'s floor, `cellRectPoly`'s inclusive
rect, `corridorPoly`'s kept first leg, `pathPoly`'s single-cell branch, `buildBrushStroke`'s
radius floor). Under Free the same gesture gives: **Room — nothing** (cited, C4);
**Corridor — nothing** (both legs degenerate, `bandRect` returns null); **N-gon — nothing**
(`snapSpan` is identity under Free, so `acrossFlats` is 0 and `ngonPoly` returns null);
**Path — a round dot** of `bandWidth` (`bufferPolyline`'s single-point `roundCap`);
**Carve — a round dot** of `width`. Three silent no-ops and two dots, from one gesture. Only
Room's half of this is cited.

**F9 — §12's exclusion of Symbol and Label is by omission, not by rule.**
`VERTEX_ATTRACT_TOOLS` is an allowlist (`vector-tools.ts:314`), and §12's prose names its
exclusions as "the cell-anchored tools (Room, Corridor, N-gon, Carve, Path — §2's list)".
Symbol and Label are not on §2's list and are not `FloorPrimitiveTool`s, so nothing in
either the spec or the test suite states why they do not attract — they simply never reach
`toLatticeSnapped` (`VectorMapView.svelte:2169`, `:2178` short-circuit to `toLatticeRaw`).
§3 below supplies the reason; it should be written down.

### Observation, deliberately *not* raised as an intake item

`corridorPoly`'s leg-existence tests are hard-disabled under Free
(`const noH = mode !== 'free' && …`, `primitives.ts:383`), so `bend` is always true there
and **both** legs are marked interior. This has no behavioural effect — `bandSpan`'s Free
branch ignores `extendA`/`extendB` entirely — and the absent leg of a straight Free drag is
dropped by `bandRect`'s degenerate-area check instead. Correct as written; noted here only
so a later reader does not re-derive it as a bug.

## 3. Should Symbol and Label join the vertex-attracting set?

**No.** Three reasons, in descending order of weight:

1. **It would break the invariant both tools exist to hold.** `anchorCellFor`
   (`symbol-catalog.ts:201-214`) and the `symbol` short-circuit in `wireStagePointerEvents`
   (`VectorMapView.svelte:2172-2178`) both state it: *the placed footprint must contain the
   clicked point*, which is why they floor rather than round. Vertex attraction moves the
   anchor to the **nearest** vertex within the pick radius — in any direction, including
   away from the click — so the footprint would stop containing the click. That is IN-014's
   bug (a symbol landing in the adjacent cell) reintroduced through a different door.
2. **The anchor is the footprint's top-left, not its centre.** The candidate set is
   `vertexHandles(regions, walls, doors)` — floor-ring vertices and wall/door endpoints.
   Attracting the top-left corner to a wall endpoint puts the symbol's *corner* on the wall
   and its body down-and-right of it. There is no reading of "flush against existing
   geometry" that this delivers.
3. **The want behind it is already served.** "Put this symbol flush in the corner of a
   room" is Cell snap, one control away and already on the toolbar for both tools
   (`SNAP_TOOLS` includes them). Free's attraction exists for the tools that have *no*
   cell-aligned mode worth using for the job; Symbol and Label do.

If it were ever genuinely wanted, the honest feature is attracting the **footprint centre**
to a vertex, or the anchor to a *cell corner* — a different rule with a different candidate
set, and a new spec section rather than an extension of §12.

**Recommended follow-through:** F9 — restate §12's exclusion as an allowlist rationale that
names Symbol and Label and gives reason (1) above, and extend `vector-tools.test.ts:918`'s
third case (`excludes Select…`) with `symbol` and `label`, so the answer is pinned rather
than implied by an allowlist's silence.

DEC-080 adds a fourth column (`grid`) to the table in §1. Three things from this audit bear
on it directly:

- The column it is joining has **three anchor families**, not two, and a hex map's tools
  will have to declare which of the three shapes each one's `grid` behaviour resembles —
  hex-vertex, hex-centre, or hex-address-floor. §1's second table is the shape of that
  question.
- **Only two tools change shape class per mode today, and both changes are cited.** A hex
  mode that adds a third uncited one would be a new outlier, not a continuation of an
  existing pattern.
- **The zero-gesture answer (F8) is the thing most likely to be got wrong per mode**, since
  it falls out of five separate floors and identity functions rather than one rule. Whatever
  `grid` does, it should answer "a click with no drag" once, explicitly, for all of them.
