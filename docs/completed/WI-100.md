# WI-100 — Free-form terrain beside per-hex terrain: the investigation

**Closed 2026-09-03.** From IN-091, against DEC-082. **Investigation — findings only, no
code changes** (DEC-027, RULE-015). Model target `opus`, run on `opus`.

DEC-082 asks whether free-form terrain and per-hex terrain are two layers or one
representation, and was postponed pending evidence on four points. This is that evidence.
Everything below was built by reading `renderHexTiles` and `renderAll`
(`apps/web/src/lib/map/vector-engine.ts`, `apps/web/src/lib/components/VectorMapView.svelte`),
`packages/shared/src/map/hex/{axial,grid,catalog}.ts`, `CampaignStore`'s hex-tile block and
`tool-groups.ts` — plus four rendered figures and one benchmark, both reproducible from the
listings in §7.

**Answer to DEC-082: (b), with two riders.** Free mode writes hex tiles; the region layer,
the migration and the rules block are not bought. §5 is the finding that moves it, and it is
not one of the four the work item asked for.

---

## 1. What the organic edge is actually worth — the figures

The gesture is one drag of a hex-sized circular brush (radius = the hex inradius, so the
brush covers about one hex), identical in every figure. What varies is `hex.size`.

**[`wi-100/edge-48.svg`](wi-100/edge-48.svg)** — hex size **48**, `DEFAULT_HEX_GRID_CONFIG`,
roughly 100% zoom. Left: the free-form region (a). Middle: the same drag written as whole
hexes (b). Right: (b) with (a) laid over it in red, so the disagreement is the only thing
you look at.

**[`wi-100/edge-20.svg`](wi-100/edge-20.svg)** — the same three panels at hex size **20**,
the zoom a hex crawl is actually read at when the referee wants the region rather than the
hex.

### What they show

At **48 the staircase is unmistakable** — (b) reads as *hexes that have been painted in*,
not as a forest. At **20 it reads as a rough-edged region**, and the difference from (a) is
a texture difference rather than a shape difference. The brush is hex-sized by definition,
so the staircase is always about one hex deep; what changes between the figures is how many
hexes the region spans, and that is what decides whether the eye reads "blocks" or "rough
edge".

Measured over the same gesture (0.75px sampling grid, `wi-100` benchmark §7):

| `hex.size` | hexes painted | area (b) vs (a) | area the two disagree on |
| ---------- | ------------- | --------------- | ------------------------ |
| 48         | 24            | **+3.5%**       | 27.4% of (a)             |
| 32         | 39            | **−1.2%**       | 38.4% of (a)             |
| 20         | 70            | **+3.7%**       | 44.2% of (a)             |

**(b) preserves the region's extent to within a few percent at every size.** All of the
disagreement is edge shape, and all of it is within one `hex.size` of the boundary — that is
not measured but guaranteed by construction: a hex is painted iff its centre is inside the
brush, and no point of a hex is more than its circumradius from its centre. **Nothing about
(b) loses where the forest is; it loses only what its edge looks like.**

The disagreement fraction *rises* as hexes get smaller, which is the opposite of the
intuition, and it is an artefact of the brush being hex-sized: at size 20 the same gesture
paints a thinner ribbon, so a one-hex-deep edge is a larger share of it. It is the wrong
number to steer by. The figures are the right evidence, and the figures say the edge is
worth something at 48 and very little at 20.

## 2. What the render-time union costs

**Confirmed against the code: it is a `renderHexTiles` change and nothing is stored.**
`renderHexTiles` (`vector-engine.ts:1185`) already clears one `Graphics` and rebuilds the
whole terrain plane from the tile list on every call, and `renderAll` calls it
unconditionally. The union is an extra pass over the same list.

**It is paid more often than DEC-082 assumes.** `renderAll()` runs from `onPointerMove`
(`VectorMapView.svelte:2920`, `:2941`) — on every pointer move, including plain hover in
Select mode with no drag in progress. So the terrain pass is a per-mouse-move cost, not a
per-edit cost, which is the right way to read the numbers below. (The full-redraw shape of
this is already logged: `DECISIONS.md` → Postponed, "Full-viewport-diff rendering
optimizations". This investigation does not re-log it.)

**The algorithm is not a polygon boolean.** A hex's six edges are each shared with exactly
one neighbour, so the boundary of a like-terrain group is "every edge whose neighbour is
a different kind", stitched into loops. That is O(n) with a hash, and **it needs no float
geometry at all**: every hex corner is an exact integer pair in *thirds* of an axial
coordinate — corner `i` of hex `(q,r)` is `(3q,3r) + CORNER_THIRDS[i]`, with
`CORNER_THIRDS = [(2,−1),(1,1),(−1,2),(−2,1),(−1,−1),(1,−2)]` — so two adjacent hexes agree
on a shared corner's *identity*, not merely on its value to within epsilon. This is SPEC-047
§1's `HexPoint` in thirds, arriving from a second direction.

Measured (Node 22, this container; geometry only, no Pixi):

| painted hexes | today's pass | + union (string keys) | + union (integer keys) |
| ------------- | ------------ | --------------------- | ---------------------- |
| 300           | 0.38 ms      | 0.91 ms               | **0.18 ms**            |
| 600           | 0.90 ms      | 1.75 ms               | **0.34 ms**            |
| 1200          | 1.55 ms      | 3.32 ms               | **0.65 ms**            |
| 2400          | 2.98 ms      | 6.59 ms               | **1.55 ms**            |
| 5000          | 6.47 ms      | 16.35 ms              | **3.01 ms**            |

Worst case — 1200 hexes with no two neighbours alike, which no hex crawl looks like —
**2.1 ms** with integer keys, 13.6 ms with string keys.

**The union is not what makes (a) untenable.** At the few-hundred-hex scale the work item
named, it costs a fifth of a millisecond per mouse move on top of a pass that already costs
twice that. Two caveats, stated rather than buried: these numbers bound the **union pass**,
which is pure JS over the tile list; they do not include Pixi's tessellation and upload,
which today's pass also pays and which the union barely adds to (3–95 extra stroked loops
across the whole run, against hundreds of filled hexes). And the 5× gap between string and
integer keys is the only tuning that matters — use the thirds pair, not a `"q,r"` string.

**One structural note.** The union is needed only for the **outline**. Like-coloured
adjacent hexes already merge visually because they are filled into one `Graphics` with one
colour; the seams DEC-082 wants gone are only visible once something is drawn along them.
So the union pass is purely additive, and it can be skipped entirely for a terrain kind that
declares no border colour.

## 3. Whether the seeded icon scatter works at hex scale

**[`wi-100/scatter.svg`](wi-100/scatter.svg)** — top row: a scatter seeded from a region id
and clipped to the region, at hex sizes 20, 32 and 48. Bottom row: today's single centred
overlay per painted hex, over the same hexes.

**It reads as texture, not as noise, at every size shown**, and the comparison is
one-sided: at size 48 the per-hex overlay reads as *a grid of repeated icons*, which is
precisely the regularity IN-091 is complaining about, while the scatter reads as woodland.
At size 20 the per-hex overlay degrades further, into a repeating stipple.

**[`wi-100/perhex.svg`](wi-100/perhex.svg)** is the finding that matters, and it was not
asked for. The scatter does **not** need a region to be seeded from. Seed it **per hex from
that hex's own axial key** — `mulberry32(hashSeed("q,r"))`, RULE-013's exact pattern — and
place a fixed count of icons inside the hex. Then:

- the texture is continuous across hex boundaries: **no hex-shaped clumping or seams are
  visible at any of the three sizes**, because a uniform scatter per hex at a constant count
  *is* a uniform scatter over the union;
- density is fixed per hex, so it is zoom-invariant by construction, which is what RULE-006
  demands of anything crossing the render boundary;
- it is **stable under editing** — painting one more hex adds that hex's icons and moves
  nothing else, where a region-seeded scatter re-rolls the whole field every time the region
  changes;
- every client derives the same field from data every client already has, with **nothing
  stored** — no region id, no seed field, no schema.

So the visual half of what (a) was buying — "a forest, not hexes with tree icons in them" —
is available under (b) at zero storage cost. Six icons per hex is the density in the figure;
it is a catalog-level number, not a stored one.

## 4. What erase means under each alternative

Under **(b)** the question dissolves: there is one layer, so erase is
`setHexTerrain(..., null)` per hex under the brush, which is the contract that already
exists — "clearing the last thing a hex carries deletes its document", so "erased back to
blank" and "never painted" stay the same state on an infinite plane
(`campaign-store.ts`, `setHexTerrain`). One gesture, one meaning, no mode-dependent rule to
document or to get wrong.

Under **(a)** it needs the split DEC-082 proposes — Hex snap clears the tile, Free snap cuts
the region — and that split is worse than it looks written down. The two erases are not
distinguishable by looking at the map: a hex showing forest may be showing its own
`terrain`, or a region beneath it, or both, and which one the referee's Free-snap erase
removes depends on a precedence rule they cannot see. Clearing a tile over a painted region
*reveals* the region rather than erasing anything, which will read as the erase having
failed. Nothing about that is unfixable, but all of it is spec surface that (b) never
generates.

## 5. The finding that moves the answer: (a)'s two layers do not compose

DEC-082's Impact section costs (a) as a collection, a migration, a rules block and store
methods. That is the cheap half. The expensive half is that **the free-form layer is
invisible to everything that reads a hex's terrain, and a map painted free-form has no
terrain data at all.**

SPEC-030 §1 made the coordinate the addressing scheme, and the terrain kind hangs off the
address: `HexTile.terrain` is what the quick sheet shows for the selected hex (§5, WI-041),
and it is what a `.vttcamp` carries. Under (a)'s precedence rule — a hex with a `terrain`
wins, a hex without shows the region beneath — a referee who paints a forest entirely in
Free mode produces a map that **looks** like a forest and in which **every hex answers "no
terrain"**: the quick sheet shows blank, the export carries nothing, and the region is a
picture rather than a record.

This is the same objection DEC-082 itself raises against alternative (c) — that a hex would
no longer *have* a terrain, it would be *inside* one — and it applies to (a) in exactly the
same way for any hex the referee never separately painted. (a) does not avoid it by keeping
`hexTiles` authoritative; it avoids it only for hexes that were painted twice.

RULE-002 means nothing in the app *interprets* terrain, so this is not a broken feature
today. It is a map that stops being able to answer a question about itself — which is the
one property SPEC-030 deliberately bought, and (b) keeps for free.

## 6. The recommendation back to DEC-082

**Answer (b): Free mode writes hex tiles at sub-hex resolution.** The brush is a UI
affordance and everything stays per-hex. No new collection, no migration, no rules block, no
export change, no precedence rule, no two-meaning erase.

Two riders, without which (b) is genuinely the worse artefact and which together cost one
catalog field and one render pass:

1. **Draw the union outline** (§2). A merged like-terrain group gets one stroked boundary,
   which is what makes it read as a region rather than as tiling. The border colour goes on
   `HexTerrainEntry` beside its fill, exactly as DEC-082 already argued. Use the thirds pair
   as the corner key, not a string.
2. **Draw the per-hex seeded scatter** (§3). It is what actually removes the "hexes with
   icons in them" reading, it is stable under editing, and it stores nothing.

**What the user is giving up, stated plainly:** the organic edge, and only at large
`hex.size`. §1's figures are the honest version of that trade — at 48 it is a real loss, at
20 it is close to invisible, and in neither case does the region move or change size.

**What would reopen it:** a referee who paints coastlines. A coastline is the one terrain
boundary whose *shape* is the content rather than the decoration, and no scatter or outline
recovers it. If that turns out to be the want, this recommendation is wrong and (a) is
right — which is a question for the user, not for the code.

## 7. Reproducing the figures and the benchmark

Both scripts were written and run in the session scratchpad and are **not committed**
(RULE-015: this work item changes no code). They are reproduced here so the numbers above
can be re-derived.

<details><summary>The union benchmark (§2)</summary>

Boundary extraction keys each corner as `(3q + CORNER_THIRDS[i].q, 3r + CORNER_THIRDS[i].r)`
and each edge `i → i+1` as shared with `AXIAL_DIRECTIONS[(i + 2) % 6]`, which is the pairing
`hexOwnedEdgePath` (`map/hex/grid.ts:115`) already documents for its NE/SE/S subset. Tiles
are a spiral of `n` hexes with terrain assigned by hex-distance Voronoi to `n/40` seeds — a
hex crawl's clumping, not a checkerboard's. Timings are the mean of 1000 iterations (200
above 2000 hexes) after one warm-up, `process.hrtime.bigint()`.

</details>

<details><summary>The figures (§§1, 3)</summary>

One normalised 27-point gesture, inset by the brush radius so the whole region is inside the
panel, rendered at `hex.size` 20/32/48 into 640×470 panels. `axialToPixel`, `hexCorners`,
`hashSeed` and `mulberry32` are transcribed from `map/hex/axial.ts` and `dice/engine.ts`
unchanged. (a) is the gesture stroked at `2 × inradius` with round caps and joins — which is
the buffer exactly. (b) is every hex whose centre is within the inradius of the polyline.
Colours are `HEX_TERRAIN_CATALOG`'s `forest`.

</details>
