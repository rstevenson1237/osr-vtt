## DEC-099 — The mobile quick-sheet chips carry a word, rather than teaching their glyphs some other way

**Closed 2026-09-18** — agent default under the `/work-item` chain (IN-135). Notified at the
WI-141 gate; overridable there.

- **Question.** On a phone the six quick sheets are a row of 16px glyphs in 44px chips whose
  only name is a `title` attribute, and touch has no hover. A player who has never seen the
  app is shown six unlabelled marks — Map tools, Character, Roll, Room, Battle map, Random
  tables — and can only learn them by tapping each one. How does that row become legible?
- **Answer. Put the word under the glyph**, the way the main-view tab bar directly above it
  already does, and take the glyph to 20px in the taller chip.
- **Why.** The answer is already in the product and one row away: `MainViewTabs` renders icon
  *and* label in both variants, and `ActivityDrawer` exists on desktop for precisely this
  reason — the README says the drawer carries icon and label "since being readable is the
  point". The chips are the only icon-only navigation surface in the app that has no hover, no
  drawer and no label. Consistency here costs one `<span>` and buys the phone shell the same
  affordance every other surface has.
- **Why not a long-press tooltip.** It teaches nothing until the player already suspects there
  is something to learn, it collides with the OS text-selection and context gestures on both
  platforms, and it leaves the resting state — the thing a new player actually looks at —
  exactly as unreadable as it is now.
- **Why not a first-run coach mark or a legend.** Both are one-time and the problem is not:
  the referee who opens the app monthly is in the same position as the new player. They also
  add a dismissal state to persist, which is real work for an affordance that disappears.
- **Why not leave it and rely on the glyphs being better.** The redraw (WI-139) genuinely
  improves them, and it does not close this gap. A glyph can only say *which* sheet once you
  know the six exist; a word says it cold.
- **Cost, stated plainly.** The chip rail grows by roughly 10px on a phone, against the
  bottom-pinned chrome SPEC-033 §§1–3 already fights to keep on screen. That budget is the one
  thing execution must measure rather than assume: if the taller rail costs the stage more than
  it is worth, the fallback is the word at 9px with the glyph staying 16px, not abandoning the
  label.
- **Impact.** `QuickSheetRail.svelte`'s `chips` variant only — one `<span>`, its CSS, and the
  chip's height. The `quick-sheet-toggle-*` testids are untouched and `mobile.spec.ts` selects
  them by testid alone, never by text or size.
- **Overridable**: yes, at the WI-141 gate.
