## DEC-076 — Icons depict the implement, not the mark and not the map-legend glyph

**Closed 2026-08-28** — answered by the user from the design canvas (option (a) of three).

- **Question.** IN-074 asks for the whole icon set to be redrawn "high user discoverable",
  mono- or bi-colour. Before any glyph could be drawn, one thing had to be settled that no
  document had ever stated: **what is an icon in this app a picture of?** SPEC-001 §4 fixes
  the technique and is silent on the subject, which is why thirty-four glyphs answered the
  question thirty-four times.
- **How it was answered.** Three directions were drawn out in full — all 33 shell and
  map-palette glyphs each, applied to the real rail, the real map palette and a phone bar —
  and compared side by side rather than argued about. The canvas is
  `https://claude.ai/code/artifact/b26abf12-6395-40ce-9234-948cac7c5e61`.
- **Answer. (a) Implement.** A glyph depicts the object a person holds or points at. Single
  colour, stroke, `currentColor`, 24 × 24, no fills. Specified as SPEC-043 §2, with the
  map-tool carve-out at §3.
- **Why not (b) Mark** — draw what appears on the map when you use the tool; two tones, the
  tint being the thing you get. It was the strongest answer to *discoverability inside the
  map palette* specifically, and the only one of the three in which **Pen and Carve look
  like different acts** (see SPEC-043 §3 for the cost of not having that). It was rejected
  on two counts: half of it has to be learned once — a dimension span means nothing until
  you have seen one — and it strains where there is no mark to draw, so Chat, Log and
  Session would have become conventional glyphs sitting inside a system that promised
  otherwise. It also contradicts SPEC-001 §4's "single-colour", so it could not have been
  scheduled without amending that spec first.
- **Why not (c) Key** — draw each glyph as a printed dungeon map's legend would cut it;
  solid masses, mitred corners, negative gaps. It has the most identity of the three and
  the highest contrast, which is the right property for a phone flat on a table. Rejected
  as too expensive to hold: solid glyphs force cuts that a stroke does not, a full palette
  of solids stops being background and competes with the map itself, and concepts with no
  legend precedent (Chat, Session) fall back to generic solids — a seam that widens with
  every icon added. Like (b), it contradicts SPEC-001 §4, which says *stroke*.
- **Impact.** One file's `MARKUP` record, 34 entries. No `IconId` added or removed, no
  chrome touched, no schema, no rules, no testids. SPEC-043 is new; SPEC-001 §4 is
  unchanged and this decision is conformant to it — which is the reason the work item
  classified Simple rather than Deceptive.
- **Overridable**: per-glyph. The rule is the decision; any individual drawing that fails
  in play is a fresh intake item against SPEC-043 §2, not a reopening of this entry.
  Changing the *rule* — adopting (b) or (c) later — is a new decision that supersedes this
  one and amends SPEC-001 §4 first.
