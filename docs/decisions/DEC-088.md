## DEC-088 — On what terms does the Worldographer terrain pack replace the current one?

_Raised by IN-114 (2026-09-08). Supersedes the art choice IN-089 made, not the terms
DEC-083 settled._

**Question.** The project owner supplies two public-domain icon sets by Inkwell Ideas,
Inc. — `bw-icons` (37 PNG) and `multicolored-classic` (60 PNG) — to replace the terrain
art WI-101 landed four days earlier. The direction is settled: the B&W set is the primary
source, the multicoloured set is a gap-filler, contents are out of scope. Three things are
not settled, and none of them can be guessed.

**What is *not* in question.** DEC-083 (i) already answered kind-string continuity —
**extend and alias, never rename in place** — and SPEC-047 §6 writes it down. Every current
terrain kind keeps resolving, to the new art where that is the intent. This decision does
not reopen it, and a migration appearing in the execution session is the signal that a kind
was renamed rather than aliased.

**Measured facts** (verified 2026-09-08 against the supplied files, not assumed):

- **`bw-icons`: 37 PNG, 300×300 RGBA, every one a single flat ink colour `#484848` with the
  antialiasing carried entirely in the alpha channel.** They are true single-tone
  silhouettes. Re-authoring to white is an RGB substitution preserving alpha — the same
  "substitution, not a redraw" DEC-083 (ii) recorded. Ships `black_white.properties` naming
  25 of the 37 with label, category and scale factor (0.82); 12 PNGs are unreferenced
  extras. **There is no water or ocean glyph** — Worldographer draws Ocean and Sea as a
  background colour with no icon, which is `HexTerrainEntry.color` arriving from another
  direction.
- **`multicolored-classic`: 60 PNG, 250×250, and 55 are multi-tone** (2–5 ink colours). A
  multiply tint cannot express two tones — DEC-083 (iii)'s finding, at 55× the scale — so
  those are a redraw, not a substitution. **Six are single-tone**: `cultivatedfarmland`,
  `deadforest`, `grassyhills`, `reefs`, `sandydesert`, `snowfields`. The owner's own example
  (farmland) is one of the six.
- **Coverage against the current 20 terrain kinds.** B&W covers `plains` (grassland),
  `forest`, `jungle`, `hills`, `mountains`, `swamp`, `desert`, `barren` (badlands),
  `reed` (marsh), `mountain-major`, `mountain-minor`, `tree-deciduous`, `tree-evergreen`.
  It has **no equivalent for `water`, `tundra`, `ice-floe`, `palm`, `plateau`**, and `grass`
  collapses onto `plains`. `snowfields` covers `tundra`/`ice-floe`. B&W adds ~20 shapes with
  no current kind — the compound `forested-`/`jungle-`/`evergreen-` hills-and-mountains
  family, `dunes`, `desert-rocky`, `cactus`, `volcano`.

**Recommendation.**

**(1) Trace to SVG; do not land PNG.** The supplied art is raster and the current refs are
SVG. `AssetStore.resolve` is extension-agnostic (its own doc comment uses `tokens/goblin.png`)
and `loadCachedTexture` hands the ref to `PIXI.Assets.load`, which loads either — so PNG
would work today with **zero code change**, and a multiply tint behaves identically on an
RGBA texture. Two things argue against taking that shortcut. **SPEC-030 §2 says "a background
colour plus an SVG overlay"** in its normative text, so landing PNG is a stated-behaviour
change to a Completed spec rather than an asset swap. And 300×300 art scaled to
`hexTerrainArtPx(48) ≈ 53px` is fine downscaled but blurs on zoom-in, where SVG stays crisp.

Tracing was **proven this session, not assumed**: `potracer` (pure Python, no system
dependency) run over the alpha mask at threshold 128 produces a single
`<path class="ink" fill="#ffffff" fill-rule="evenodd">` per icon — 2–17 subpaths, 16–148
segments, 0.6–5.0 KB, **mostly smaller than the source PNG**. `class="ink"` is the convention
the existing pack already carries, so the traced files land in the shape `catalog.ts` and
SPEC-047 §6 already describe. The trace threshold is the one judgement call and belongs in the
execution session's summary.

**(2) Take the ~20 new B&W shapes as new kinds now, in the same item.** They are additive —
a new row in `HEX_TERRAIN_CATALOG` with a `kind`, a `label`, a `color` and a `ref`, reachable
from WI-041's palette, breaking nothing that exists. Deferring them means tracing the same
files twice and tuning the same palette twice, and the palette is the part that needs a human
eye. The counter-argument is real and is why this is a decision rather than a default: the
terrain palette goes from 20 kinds to ~40, and the hex-tile sheet's palette has never been
shown that many. If the palette needs grouping (the properties file's own `category` —
Forests, Rough Land, Other Land, Water — is a ready-made grouping), that is a UI change and
should be its own intake item, not smuggled in here.

**(3) `volcano` becomes `volcano-terrain` in the terrain catalog; the contents kind keeps its
name.** B&W ships a `volcano` terrain glyph and `HEX_CONTENTS_CATALOG` already has a `volcano`
contents kind. The two catalogs are separate maps and a duplicate string is not a collision in
code — a hex can carry `terrain: 'volcano'` and `contents: 'volcano'` and both resolve
correctly. It is a collision for a *reader*, and for anyone writing the palette UI or a future
export. Renaming the **new** kind costs nothing (nothing is stored under it yet); renaming the
**existing** contents kind is exactly the rename DEC-083 (i) forbids.

**Impact.** With the recommendation: no schema change, no migration, no store-contract change,
no rules change, no coordinate-space change. `HEX_TERRAIN_CATALOG` is rewritten,
~40 traced `.svg` files land under `apps/web/public/assets/hex/terrain/`, WI-101's 20 terrain
files are deleted (their kinds keep resolving, to new art), `ATTRIBUTION.md` gains the
Inkwell Ideas entry and its hex-terrain paragraph is corrected, and SPEC-030 §2 and SPEC-047
§6 are annotated. `HEX_CONTENTS_CATALOG` and its 26 files are untouched.

**The reversible and irreversible halves.** The trace is reversible — the sources are kept at
`docs/intake/`, and re-tracing at a different threshold is a script re-run. The **palette
colours are not**: `HEX_TERRAIN_CATALOG`'s colours were hand-tuned against WI-101's glyphs,
with a stated reason (mid-tone, so a fill neither swallows a black contents icon nor bleaches
the overlay), and ~20 new kinds need ~20 new colours chosen to the same constraint. That is
the manual work in this item, and doing it twice is what recommendation (2) avoids.

**What it does to the two open terrain items.** **IN-105** (a per-region border colour on
`HexTerrainEntry`) and **IN-106** (per-hex seeded scatter in place of the centred overlay)
both want to change this entry's shape and this overlay's meaning. Neither is blocked by this
decision and neither blocks it — but IN-106 in particular gets *easier* with the B&W pack,
because these glyphs are single motifs at a consistent weight rather than composed scenes.
Worth noting, not worth coupling.

**Alternatives.**

(a) *Recommended, above.* Trace to SVG, take the new kinds now, rename the new terrain
`volcano`.

(b) **Land the PNGs as-is.** Zero code change, zero tracing risk, art preserved exactly.
Costs SPEC-030 §2's "SVG overlay" wording — a stated-behaviour amendment to a Completed spec —
and blurs on zoom. Cheapest, and the one to pick if the trace fidelity disappoints on the
denser glyphs (`forest-heavy`, `forest-mixed-mountains`) when they are actually looked at.

(c) **Swap art for the existing 20 kinds only; defer every new kind.** The smallest possible
change and the closest reading of "no huge change here". Leaves `water`, `palm` and `plateau`
pointing at art that has no B&W equivalent — so either they keep WI-101's glyphs (two packs
side by side, which is the outcome this item exists to end) or they go to `snowfields`-style
substitutes that do not mean the same thing. Re-tunes the palette twice.

(d) **Take the multicoloured set as the primary and add an untinted-art path to the pipeline.**
What "multicoloured classic" would buy if the pipeline allowed it. Rejected on the same
grounds DEC-083 (iii) rejected it for one file: it costs SPEC-030 §2's contrast guarantee,
which is what stops a re-coloured terrain from orphaning its overlay, and it is now 55 files
rather than one.

**Answer.** **(a), with the roster settled — user, 2026-09-08.**

- **Trace to SVG**, as recommended.
- **Take all 37 B&W shapes**, the 12 unreferenced extras included. The B&W set defines the
  roster.
- **Four additions from the multicoloured set**, the ones that are single-tone *and* not
  duplicates of a B&W shape: `cultivatedfarmland`, `snowfields`, `deadforest`, `reefs`.
  `sandydesert` and `grassyhills` are held back pending the reference sheet, which shows
  whether they differ from B&W's own `desert` and `grassland` enough to earn a kind.
- **Kinds with no equivalent leave the palette but keep resolving** — `palm` and `plateau`
  outright, `grass` onto `grassland` and `scrub` onto `cactus`. No kind is deleted, so no
  migration. This is the alias rule read as "unpaintable", not "gone".
- **`water` is not a gap.** It becomes **three shades of blue, background only, no glyph** —
  which is how Worldographer draws Ocean and Sea (its own B&W config gives them `#999999`
  and `#cccccc` backgrounds and no icon, because the set is monochrome; the blues are this
  project's own choice). `tundra` and `ice-floe` resolve to `snowfields`.
- **Names come from the filenames**, minus the `bw-` prefix, rather than from
  `black_white.properties`' labels. One wrinkle for the reference sheet: the properties file
  labels `bw-brokenlands.png` "Badlands" while `bw-badlands.png` is an unreferenced extra, so
  filename-canon yields both `badlands` and `brokenlands` as distinct kinds.
- **`volcano` stays in both catalogs.** Not renamed. Two catalogs, two lookups, no added
  work — and the two readings are genuinely different: the terrain says *volcanic country*,
  the contents kind says *that volcano, and it is interesting*.
- **The ~42-kind palette is grouped**, and the grouping is designed on the reference sheet
  rather than logged as a separate item.

**And a change of remit the answer brought with it:** the colour model is no longer
"white ink on a background". See **DEC-089**, raised by this answer.
