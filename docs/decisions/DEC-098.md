## DEC-098 — Icon render size is three stops chosen by pointer coarseness, not a number each caller picks

**Closed 2026-09-18** — agent default under the `/work-item` chain (IN-138). Notified at the
WI-139 gate; overridable there.

- **Question.** Thirteen call sites pass `Icon.svelte` a `size` prop, and between them they
  pass **six different numbers**: 14, 15, 16, 18, 19 and 20. The prop's own default is 20 and
  no caller uses it. At 14 — the mobile top bar's session gear and full-screen toggle — a
  1.75 stroke lands on roughly one device pixel and the gear's teeth close up, which is the
  mechanical reason that glyph reads as an asterisk on a phone. What should the set be drawn
  to, and who decides the number?
- **Answer. Three stops — 16, 20, 24 — and the shell picks between them by pointer
  coarseness, not the caller.** `sizing.css` gains `--icon: 20px` beside the existing `--hit`,
  dropping to `--icon-sm: 16px` for dense rows and rising to `24px` under
  `@media (pointer: coarse)` for anything a fingertip aims at. `Icon.svelte`'s `size` prop
  stays for the handful of genuinely fixed cases but its default becomes the token, so a
  caller that passes nothing is correct by construction.
- **Why.** The device split already exists and is already expressed exactly this way:
  `sizing.css` picks `--hit` (34px precise, 44px coarse) on `(pointer: coarse)` alone, never
  on screen width, so a touchscreen laptop gets touch sizing and a narrow mouse-driven window
  does not. An icon inside one of those targets has the same problem and deserves the same
  answer rather than a second, differently-shaped one. Three stops is also what makes the
  redraw checkable: a glyph is drawn to survive its smallest stop, and "survives 16px" is an
  assertion a reviewer can make, where "survives whatever the caller passed" is not.
- **Why not keep per-caller numbers.** It is the status quo and it is what produced six sizes
  for one stroke weight. Nothing warns when a fourteenth caller invents a seventh number, and
  the sizes that hurt (14 and 15) are precisely the ones a caller chose to make something fit.
- **Why not two stops.** 16 and 24 alone would push every desktop rail control to one or the
  other: 16 is too small for a 34px rail toggle that is the primary navigation, and 24 crowds
  it. 20 is the size the desktop rail already effectively uses (19), so the middle stop costs
  nothing and is where most of the set lives.
- **Why not scale with the viewport.** Screen width picks the *layout* in this app and pointer
  coarseness picks the *sizing* (SPEC-033 §7, DEC-052). Sizing an icon by width would put a
  third rule in a place that has deliberately had one.
- **Impact.** `theme/sizing.css` gains two tokens; `Icon.svelte`'s default changes; the
  thirteen call sites drop or change their `size` prop. No testid, no schema, no store, no
  rules. Visible change: the mobile top bar's two controls grow from 14 to 24 on a phone, and
  the desktop tabs move 15 → 16 and the rail 19 → 20.
- **Overridable**: yes, at the WI-139 gate, and per-stop afterwards — changing one number is a
  token edit, not a reopening. Abandoning the token and returning to per-caller numbers is a
  new decision that supersedes this one.
